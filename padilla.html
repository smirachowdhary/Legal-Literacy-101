Redefined Legal Counsel Duties

By Aanya Bhaskarabhatta | March 2026

Justice Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court.

“Under what circumstances could a deportation be ordered as a consequence” is a question that doesn’t have a single answer. There are many examples of individuals of a variety of different immigration backgrounds facing the extreme outcome of removal in trials of unrelated crimes. In complex and unknown territory like this, it’s crucial that a defendant’s legal counsel makes them aware of any potential threats to their status in the United States.

The case Padilla v. Kentucky, 559 U.S. 356, serves as a reminder of what happens when this rule is ignored. In 2009, Jose Padilla was told by his legal counsel that his drug charges wouldn’t affect his immigration status, so he chose to plead guilty. However, he was misadvised, and very quickly he found himself facing deportation, despite being a lawful resident for over forty years and a veteran in the Vietnam War.

What made Padilla’s case so significant was that the inaccurate legal counsel was in violation of the Constitution. The Sixth Amendment, which applies to all individuals regardless of citizen status, states that the defendant has the right “to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation…and to have the Assistance of Counsel for his defense”. Padilla argued that his counsel failed to “inform him of the nature” of his case, which includes the potential outcomes of his defense. If he had been aware that a guilty plea would make him eligible for deportation, he would have gone to trial instead, which leads to a very different outcome. However, the Kentucky Supreme Court believed that deportation was collateral damage that came with the ruling and wasn’t worthy of compensation.

On March 31st, 2010, the U.S. Supreme Court sided with Padilla, reaffirming the duty of legal counsels to advise non-citizens on all the possible outcomes of pleading guilty that could affect their immigration status in the United States, especially when it carries a risk of deportation.

It’s important to remember whether a citizen or not, everyone is entitled to fair representation in the court of law. Violating these Constitutional rights results in unforeseen consequences and the upending of everyday people’s lives, making it all the more crucial to understand which rights can be invoked by all individuals and the extent to which they apply.
