The Right to Education: Why Millions of Children Still Can't Go to School

By Smira Chowdhary | July 2026

Education is one of the most powerful tools for improving lives, reducing poverty, and creating opportunity. Yet more than 244 million children and youth around the world remain out of school because of poverty, conflict, discrimination, and lack of educational resources. According to UNESCO, this global crisis prevents millions of children from reaching their full potential and keeps families trapped in cycles of hardship.

Education Is a Human Right

The right to education is recognized by the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and numerous international agreements developed by the United Nations and UNESCO. These documents affirm that every child deserves access to free, quality education regardless of their background, nationality, or economic status.

Barriers to Education

Many children cannot attend school because schools lack classrooms, qualified teachers, textbooks, clean water, food, or safe facilities. UNESCO estimates that the world faces a shortage of approximately 44 million teachers, forcing some educators to teach classrooms containing over 100 students.

Although international law supports free primary and secondary education, many schools still charge enrollment fees or require families to purchase materials they simply cannot afford. For children living in extreme poverty, these costs often make education impossible.

Girls and Students with Disabilities

Millions of girls continue to face barriers to education because of gender discrimination, early marriage, household responsibilities, or inadequate menstrual hygiene resources. It is estimated that nearly 9 million primary school-aged girls will never attend school.

Children with disabilities also face significant discrimination. Worldwide, children with disabilities are substantially more likely than their peers to never enroll in school because of inaccessible buildings, lack of accommodations, or social stigma.

Education During Conflict

For children living in conflict zones, attending school can become impossible. Wars often destroy schools, displace families, and make daily survival the highest priority. Children living in places affected by armed conflict, including Gaza and other regions experiencing violence, frequently lose access to safe education because schools are damaged, occupied, or unsafe to attend.

How Can We Help?

Governments can expand access to education by increasing funding, training more teachers, and ensuring schools remain free and accessible. International organizations and nonprofits such as UNICEF and Room to Read also provide critical educational resources to children living in poverty or humanitarian crises.

Although significant challenges remain, progress is possible through cooperation between governments, educators, nonprofits, and communities. Ensuring every child has access to education helps create healthier, more prosperous, and more peaceful societies.

Why It Matters

Education is more than attending school—it gives children the knowledge, skills, and opportunities needed to build brighter futures. Protecting the right to education is one of the most important investments society can make for future generations.
