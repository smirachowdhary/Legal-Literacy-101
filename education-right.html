The Right to Education: Why Millions of Children Still Can't Go to School

By Smira Chowdhary | July 2026

Children attending school in Kenya.

Education is one of the most powerful tools for breaking the cycle of poverty, promoting equality, and creating opportunities for future generations. Yet more than 244 million children and youth around the world remain out of school because of poverty, conflict, discrimination, and a lack of educational resources. According to UNESCO, this global crisis prevents millions of children from reaching their full potential and keeps families trapped in cycles of hardship.

The right to education is recognized under Article 26 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and many other international agreements adopted through the United Nations and UNESCO. These legal frameworks recognize that every child deserves free, quality education regardless of nationality, economic status, gender, or disability.

Despite these protections, many children are unable to attend school because of inadequate infrastructure, overcrowded classrooms, shortages of qualified teachers, and limited access to books, clean water, food, and other basic necessities. UNESCO estimates that the world faces a shortage of nearly 44 million teachers, forcing some educators to teach classes of more than one hundred students. In many countries, enrollment fees or required school supplies also create financial barriers, even though international law supports free primary and secondary education.

Girls and children with disabilities face additional obstacles. In many communities, gender stereotypes, early marriage, household responsibilities, and inadequate menstrual hygiene resources prevent girls from completing their education. It is estimated that nearly 9 million girls of primary school age will never attend school. Likewise, children with disabilities are significantly more likely than their peers to be excluded from education because schools may lack accessible facilities, trained teachers, or necessary accommodations.

Armed conflict creates another major barrier to education. Children living in places affected by war, including Gaza and other conflict-affected regions, often cannot attend school because buildings have been destroyed, families have been displaced, or daily survival has become the highest priority. Schools are frequently among the first pieces of infrastructure damaged during conflict, leaving children without safe places to learn.

Although these challenges are significant, progress is possible. Governments can increase education funding, hire and train more teachers, and eliminate financial barriers that prevent children from attending school. International organizations and nonprofits, including UNICEF and Room to Read, also play an essential role by providing educational resources and support to children living in poverty or humanitarian crises.

Education is more than simply attending school—it is a fundamental human right that empowers children to build brighter futures for themselves and their communities. Protecting this right requires continued investment, international cooperation, and a commitment to ensuring that every child, regardless of where they are born, has the opportunity to learn.
